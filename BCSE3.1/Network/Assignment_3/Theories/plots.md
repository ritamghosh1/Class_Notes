# CSMA Simulation Plot Explanations

This document provides a detailed breakdown of the 9 benchmark plots generated by `plot_results.py`. The plots are divided into two main benchmarking sweeps: a **probability ($p$) sweep** for p-Persistent CSMA, and a **station count ($N$) sweep** comparing all four protocols.

### p-Persistent Sweep (Fixed $N=10$)

**1. `fig1_p_collisions.png` (Collisions vs. Probability $p$)**
- **Explanation:** This plot demonstrates the direct correlation between the persistence parameter $p$ and the frequency of channel collisions. As $p$ approaches 1, stations become overly eager to transmit immediately after sensing an idle channel, leading to a dramatic, almost exponential increase in collisions.

**2. `fig2_p_delay.png` (Average Delay vs. Probability $p$)**
- **Explanation:** This illustrates the inverse relationship between persistence probability and latency. At very low $p$ values (e.g., $p=0.1$), stations frequently defer their transmissions even when the channel is idle, resulting in high average wait times. As $p$ increases, stations transmit sooner, dropping the delay metric.

**3. `fig3_p_throughput.png` (Throughput vs. Probability $p$)**
- **Explanation:** This is the critical trade-off curve for p-Persistent CSMA. It reveals the optimal $p$ value (typically around $0.1$ to $0.3$). If $p$ is too low, the channel sits idle unnecessarily. If $p$ is too high, throughput collapses due to constant collision saturation.

### Protocol Comparison Sweep (Varying $N$)

**4. `fig4_n_collisions.png` (Collisions vs. Number of Stations $N$)**
- **Explanation:** A cross-protocol comparison showing how collisions scale with network load. **1-Persistent CSMA** suffers the steepest and highest increase, as multiple waiting stations will deterministically collide the moment a current transmission finishes. **CSMA/CD** effectively limits collision duration by aborting early, and **Non-Persistent** scatters re-transmission attempts via random backoffs.

**5. `fig5_n_delay.png` (Average Delay vs. Number of Stations $N$)**
- **Explanation:** Highlights how queuing delays and Binary Exponential Backoff (BEB) timers grow as network contention increases. **1-Persistent** often has the lowest delay at *low* loads, but delay skyrockets at high loads due to relentless collisions forcing retries.

**6. `fig6_n_throughput.png` (Throughput vs. Number of Stations $N$)**
- **Explanation:** The definitive performance metric graph. It clearly highlights the "saturation point" of each protocol. While **1-Persistent** provides excellent throughput at $N < 5$, its performance crashes toward zero under high contention. In contrast, **CSMA/CD** and **Non-Persistent** degrade gracefully and sustain moderate throughput even at $N=30$.

### Summaries & Theoretical Models

**7. `fig7_combined_summary.png` (Combined 2x3 Panel)**
- **Explanation:** A high-resolution, comprehensive dashboard containing Figures 1 through 6 in a single grid. This is generated specifically for easy insertion into formal lab reports or presentations to summarize the entire empirical benchmark at a glance.

**8. `fig8_theoretical_throughput.png` (Theoretical S vs G)**
- **Explanation:** Plots the theoretical Throughput ($S$) against Offered Load ($G$) using classical mathematical approximations (assuming a normalized propagation delay $a = 0.01$). This plot serves as the ground-truth baseline to verify that our discrete-event simulation's empirical results align with standard networking queue theory.

**9. `fig9_p_heatmap.png` (p-Persistent Heatmap $p \times N$)**
- **Explanation:** A 2D color heatmap visualizing the "sweet spot" for tuning the parameter $p$ based on the expected number of active stations $N$. The bright regions (yellow/white) indicate the combinations of $p$ and $N$ that yield the absolute optimal throughput, showing that as $N$ increases, the optimal $p$ must correspondingly decrease.
